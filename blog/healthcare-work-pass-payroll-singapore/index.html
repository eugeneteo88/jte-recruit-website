The work pass is approved. The payroll side is the part nobody warned you about

Licensing gets the attention. Levies, CPF rules and filings are where employers actually trip.

Eugene Teo

Co-Founder, JTE Recruit · 23 December 2026 · 6 min read

When you bring healthcare staff in on a work pass, most of the attention goes to the obvious hurdles, the pass application, the registration with the right board, the credentials. Fair enough, those matter. But they are not usually where employers come unstuck.

The part that catches people out is the payroll side that starts the moment that person is on your books. It works differently for pass holders than for local staff, and the differences are exactly the kind that cost money if you assume they are the same. Here is what to sort before the first pay run.

CPF works differently for pass holders

Start with the one that surprises people most. CPF is not payable for foreigners on work passes. It is due for employees who are Singapore Citizens or PRs. So for your pass-holding healthcare staff, you are not making employer CPF contributions on their wages.

That sounds like less work, and in one narrow sense it is. But do not let it lull you, because pass holders come with a different obligation in CPF's place, and that one does cost you monthly.

Levies and quotas, the part that costs monthly

Depending on the pass type, you may owe a monthly foreign worker levy for each pass holder, and you may be subject to a quota that caps how many pass holders you can employ relative to your local staff. The levy is a real recurring cost that has to be budgeted and paid on time, and the rules differ by pass type and by sector.

This is where healthcare employers who have only ever hired locals get a shock. The headline salary is not the whole cost. The levy sits on top, every month, per worker. Rates and quotas change and vary by pass, so confirm the current figures with MOM before you model the cost of a hire.

The filings do not stop at the payslip

Pass holders are still employees, so the ordinary employer duties apply. You issue proper itemised payslips, you keep records, and you report on IR8A at year end like any other staff member.

There is one more that locals do not trigger. When a foreign employee stops working for you or leaves Singapore, you generally have to do tax clearance, the IR21 process, and you may need to withhold money until it is cleared. Miss that step and it becomes your liability, not theirs. It is easy to forget because it only happens at the end, often months after you last thought about the paperwork.

Why this trips good employers

None of this is exotic. It trips people because it is different in small, specific ways from hiring a local, and because the costs and steps show up at different times. The levy hits monthly. The CPF difference is day one. The IR21 lands at the exit. If your admin setup assumes every hire is the same, one of these slips.

And in healthcare, where you are often hiring under time pressure to cover a real gap on the floor, the payroll setup is the thing that gets rushed. That is exactly when a levy gets mis-budgeted or an IR21 gets missed.

The simpler route: let us be the employer

If you would rather not hold all of this in your head for every pass holder, we can be the employer of record. The staff do your work, in your setting, but the employment sits with us. We handle the levies, the payslips, the records, the IR8A, and the IR21 tax clearance when someone leaves.

You get the people you need without building a payroll and compliance function around work passes. For most employers we take on, this is near zero cost, because we run payroll free for the first year. One less thing to rush when you are hiring under pressure.

The takeaway The pass getting approved is the start of the payroll work, not the end of it, sort the levy, the CPF difference and the exit filing before the first pay run.

Frequently asked questions

Do I pay CPF for healthcare staff on a work pass?

No. CPF is due for employees who are Citizens or PRs, not for foreigners on work passes. Instead, pass holders may attract a monthly levy. Confirm current rates with MOM and the CPF Board.

What is the foreign worker levy?

A monthly charge an employer pays for certain pass types, on top of salary, per worker. It is a recurring cost to budget. Rates and the pass types it applies to change, so confirm the current figures with MOM.

Is there a limit on how many pass holders I can hire?

For some pass types, yes, a quota that caps pass-holder headcount relative to local staff and varies by sector. Check the current quota rules with MOM before planning headcount.

What is IR21 and when do I need it?

IR21 is tax clearance for a foreign employee who stops working for you or leaves Singapore. You generally file it and may need to withhold money until it clears. Missing it becomes your liability, so build it into your offboarding.

Do pass holders still get payslips and IR8A?

Yes. They are employees, so itemised payslips, kept records and year-end IR8A reporting all apply, the same as for any staff member.
