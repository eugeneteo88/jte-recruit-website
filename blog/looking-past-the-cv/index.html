Ten years of hiring taught me to look past the CV

Eugene Teo

Co-Founder, JTE Recruit · 7 July 2026 · 4 min read

In over ten years of hiring, I've sat across from more people than I can count. Fresh graduates and thirty-year veterans, the quietly brilliant and the naturally loud, people from just about every background and walk of life you can imagine. Every one of them brought something different to the table. And if there's one thing that decade has changed my mind about, it's how you actually tell whether someone is right for a role.

The old checklist

For a long time, hiring came down to three things: the degree, the years of experience, and, if we're honest, the age. It was a tidy checklist. Easy to screen for, easy to justify to a client. A strong CV got you into the room, and a gap in it often kept you out. Those things still matter, and I won't pretend they tell you nothing. But they tell you far less than they used to.

Why the CV means less now

The work itself moves faster than it did. Tools change, processes change, whole ways of doing a job can turn over within a year or two. So the candidate who looks flawless on paper can quietly struggle if they can't adjust, while someone with a lighter CV can outperform the whole room simply because they pick things up quickly and aren't precious about changing how they work.

What I really look for: adaptability

These days, the first thing I'm assessing is adaptability. Can this person take a new idea on board, judge honestly whether it's actually feasible, and then find the most efficient way to make it happen? I don't mean chasing every shiny new method for the sake of it. I mean the instinct to look at how something is being done and think, "there's a simpler, cleaner way to get there." That instinct is worth more to a team than almost any single line on a CV.

Attitude hasn't gone anywhere

None of this means attitude has fallen out of fashion. Far from it. You still spend your days alongside colleagues, and how you carry yourself, how you handle a setback, how you get on with the people around you, matters as much as it ever did. Skills get someone through the door. Attitude is what keeps them, and their team, moving.

Skills get someone through the door. Adaptability decides how far they go.

The measure of a good hire has flipped

Here's the biggest shift I've noticed. It used to be that the best hire was whoever could do the most. Sheer volume. Now it's whoever can finish what's actually in front of them quickly and cleanly. Efficiency has overtaken output. Doing the right things well, with the least wasted effort, beats doing the most things and drowning in all of them.

It's mindset, not generation

Before anyone says this is a generational thing, I don't buy that. It's easy to credit "the young ones" or blame "the old guard," but I've met twenty-two-year-olds stuck firmly in their ways and people in their fifties genuinely hungry to try something new. It was never really about age. It's mindset. It's mentality. And that's the part a birth year on a CV can never tell you.

What this means when we hire for you

So when we assess someone for a client today, of course we read the CV. But what I'm really listening for is how they think, how they adapt, and whether they'll leave the team better than they found it. That part is harder to screen for, and honestly, it's where a good recruiter earns their keep. If you're hiring and you'd rather judge people on more than a résumé, that's exactly the kind of conversation we enjoy.
