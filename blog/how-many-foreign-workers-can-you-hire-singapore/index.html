How many foreign workers can you actually hire?

Eugene Teo

Co-Founder, JTE Recruit · 8 September 2026 · 5 min read

A friend of mine started his own business about seven months ago.

Last month he found someone he wanted to hire — a foreigner an ex-colleague had recommended, someone he’d worked with before and trusted. Then he hit a wall: he couldn’t hire the guy. Not yet, anyway. So he gave me a call and we had a proper chat about it.

What I explained to him is something a lot of business owners only find out the hard way — hiring foreign in Singapore isn’t really about the person. It comes down to one number most owners have never heard of, and once you get it, a lot of confusing rules suddenly make sense. So here’s what I walked him through.

You don’t “get” foreign headcount. You earn it.

Here’s the whole game in one line: Singapore ties how many foreign workers you can hire to how many locals you already employ. Hire more Singaporeans and PRs, and you unlock more foreign quota. Employ no locals, and you get almost nothing.

So when MOM told my friend “not yet,” it wasn’t about his candidate at all. He simply hadn’t built the local base to carry another foreign hire.

The one number: Local Qualifying Salary

A local only counts toward your quota if you pay them enough. That threshold is the Local Qualifying Salary (LQS), and here’s how it works:

Pay a local $1,800 a month or more → counts as one local.
Pay $900 to under $1,800 → counts as half.
Pay under $900 → doesn’t count at all.

Only Singaporeans and PRs on a proper employment contract count — not you as the owner. Those are the 2026 figures, and MOM nudges them up over time.

The ceiling — why two locals won’t get you ten foreign

On top of that, every sector caps how much of your total workforce can be foreign. It’s called the Dependency Ratio Ceiling, and services is the tightest — a maximum of 35% foreign. Within that, S Pass holders can only be 10% of your workforce.

Put real numbers on it: to be allowed roughly 100 foreign workers in services, you’d need somewhere north of 180 locals on real salaries first. That’s not a couple of admin hires — it’s a genuine local workforce. Think of foreign quota as something you earn by employing locals.

EP, S Pass, Work Permit — the 30-second version

The three passes aren’t interchangeable, and only two of them eat into that quota. The Employment Pass is for higher earners (from about $5,600 a month, more with age) — no quota, no levy, so it doesn’t touch your foreign ceiling at all. The S Pass is for mid-skilled roles, minimum $3,600 a month in services; it counts toward your quota, carries a monthly levy, and is capped at 10% of your workforce. The Work Permit is for semi-skilled roles — also counts toward quota, also levied, with rules by sector.

So if you’re trying to grow foreign headcount, the EP is the “free” one; it’s the S Pass and Work Permit numbers that hit the ceiling. I’ve written a fuller breakdown of which pass fits which hire if you want the detail.

The part most owners miss: you can be paid to hire the locals

Building that local base sounds expensive. But here’s what a lot of people don’t realise — if you’re hiring a Singaporean into a genuinely new role (a career-switcher moving into admin, BD or operations), the Career Conversion Programme can co-fund up to 70% of their salary. Up to 90% if they’re 40 or older, or were out of work for six months or more.

Read that again: the local base that unlocks your foreign quota can be part-funded by the government. That changes the maths completely.

One honest line I have to say

All of this only works with real jobs. Putting names on payroll to pad your headcount, or claiming salary grants for roles that don’t exist, is exactly what MOM comes down on. Do it properly — real people, real work — and it’s completely legitimate. And honestly, it’s smart.

That’s the puzzle we untangle for clients every week — working out your quota, the local base you need, getting the passes and payroll set up right, and pointing you to the grants you actually qualify for. If you’re staring at the same wall my friend hit, send me your numbers and I’ll tell you straight what’s possible.
