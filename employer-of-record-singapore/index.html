The basics

What is an EOR in Singapore?

An Employer of Record is a company that becomes the legal employer of your staff, on paper, while you stay the real boss who hires, manages and pays for them. In Singapore that EOR has to be a properly licensed local company, because it is the one signing the employment contract, paying CPF and dealing with MOM, CPF and IRAS.

Here is the split in plain terms. You decide who to hire, what they do, how much they earn and when they move on. The EOR holds the employment contract, runs the monthly payroll, pays the CPF, issues the payslips, files the taxes and sponsors the work pass if the person is a foreigner. Your team never feels it. For them it is a normal job with a normal payslip; the paperwork just sits with us.

People reach for an EOR for one simple reason: you can employ someone in Singapore without first incorporating your own company here. No Pte Ltd, no corporate secretary, no payroll software, no year-one overhead. That is the whole appeal.

Quick note on wording. If you already have a Singapore company and just want someone else to run the payroll, that is usually called payroll outsourcing. If you don't have an entity and need a company to employ the staff for you, that is EOR. Same team, same filings, just a different starting point.

The money

What does an EOR cost in Singapore?

Commercial EORs usually charge one of two ways: a flat monthly fee per employee, or a percentage of each person's salary. That fee sits on top of the actual salary and the statutory employer costs, which you pay either way. So the real monthly bill for one hire is salary, plus employer CPF and levies, plus the EOR's fee.

The statutory part is fixed by the rules, not by any provider. For a local or PR employee, the main pieces an employer pays on top of salary look like this (always confirm the current figure with the CPF Board and MOM):

Employer statutory costs, in brief
Employer CPF (employee aged 55 and below)17%
Skills Development Levy (SDL)0.25%
SDL per employee, per monthS$2 to S$11.25
All-in employer add-on (S$800–4,500 band)~17.25%

Employer CPF of 17% applies to employees aged 55 and below and holds from 1 Jan 2026. SDL is 0.25% of monthly wages, minimum S$2 and maximum S$11.25. Figures as at Nov 2026; confirm the current rate with the CPF Board, IRAS and MOM.

So for a local hire earning, say, S$3,500 a month, an employer is looking at roughly S$595 in CPF and about S$8.75 in SDL on top, before any EOR fee. The EOR fee is the extra you pay a provider to carry the employment and do all the admin.

Hiring a foreigner? Then there is a salary floor to clear too. As at Nov 2026 the Employment Pass minimum qualifying salary is S$5,600 (rising to S$6,000 from 1 Jan 2027, and S$6,600 for the financial services sector), and the S Pass minimum is S$3,300 (rising to S$3,600 from 1 Jan 2027). Those are the person's salary, not a fee, but they shape your budget. Confirm current thresholds with MOM.

The real question

EOR vs setting up your own entity, which is cheaper?

Short answer: for your first one to a handful of hires, an EOR is usually cheaper and far less hassle. Once you have a sizeable local team and you know you're staying, your own entity tends to win. The crossover is the whole decision.

Your own Pte Ltd is not just the incorporation fee. It is a corporate secretary, accounting, annual returns, payroll software and the time you spend running all of it, every single month, before you have paid a single salary. An EOR folds that whole pile into one fee, so you skip the setup entirely and start hiring this week.

Peace of mind

Is it legal and compliant?

Yes, completely. Having a licensed local company employ staff and run their payroll is a normal, above-board arrangement in Singapore. The key word is licensed: the company carrying the employment has to be the real deal, keep CPF current and play straight with MOM and IRAS.

That is exactly where the risk hides if you get it wrong. Pay CPF late or short and you're into interest and enforcement. Miss an IR21 and a leaving employee's final pay gets held up. Keep someone on the wrong or an expired work pass and that's an offence, with fines and debarment, not just a warning. A good EOR takes all of that off your plate.
