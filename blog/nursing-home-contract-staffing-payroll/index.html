Your nursing home needs more hands, not more payroll admin

Contract care staff let you flex headcount. The payroll underneath does not have to be yours.

Eugene Teo

Co-Founder, JTE Recruit · 18 December 2026 · 6 min read

Nursing homes live and die on staffing. A ward gets busier, an outbreak hits, a few people resign at once, and suddenly you need care assistants, nurses and support staff faster than you can hire permanent heads. So you bring in contract staff. Sensible.

The part that catches managers out is everything that comes after you say yes to the extra hands. Each new worker means a contract, a payslip, CPF where it applies, levies for pass holders, records, and a year-end filing. Flex your headcount a few times and your small admin team is now running a payroll operation they never signed up for.

Why temp care staff create a payroll problem

The appeal of contract staff is that you can scale up and down. The problem is that payroll does not scale down as easily as a roster does. Every person you add, even for six weeks, has to be employed properly, paid on time, given an itemised payslip, and accounted for at year end.

Do that for two extra people and it is manageable. Do it for fifteen, across start dates that keep shifting, with some on passes and some not, and the paperwork starts to eat the very time you were trying to free up on the floor.

CPF, levies and passes are not all the same

Care teams are mixed, and the payroll rules are not uniform across them. CPF is due for employees who are Citizens or PRs. It is not due for workers on work passes. Pass holders instead come with their own employer obligations, including a monthly levy for certain pass types, and quota rules that vary by sector.

So a single roster of fifteen can carry three or four different sets of employer obligations running at once. Get one wrong and it is not a rounding error, it is unpaid CPF or a levy issue with penalties attached. The exact rates, quotas and pass rules change, so confirm the current figures with MOM and the CPF Board before you commit numbers.

The in-house HR trap

The instinct is to put a person on it. Hire HR, or push it onto the ward manager, or stretch the one admin colleague who already does everything. That works until the next surge, then it does not.

Experienced payroll HR will not take a short contract, and you should not hire a permanent HR head to handle work that spikes and fades. So you end up with the wrong person on the task, which is how payslips go out late and CPF gets missed. The staffing was meant to relieve pressure, not add a new kind.

One invoice instead of fifteen pay runs

Here is the cleaner model. We employ the contract staff as the employer of record. We run their payroll, handle CPF where it is due, manage the levies for pass holders, issue the payslips, keep the records, and do the year-end filings. You get one invoice.

When the surge passes, you wind the headcount down, and it winds down cleanly, no awkward terminations to administer, no trailing paperwork. You flex the people. We absorb the admin. Your team goes back to running the home instead of running a pay cycle.

What it costs you

For most homes we take on, the payroll side runs at near zero, because we run payroll free for the first year. You are paying for care hours, not for an admin burden on top of them.

If you are already juggling a mixed roster of permanent and contract staff and the pay runs are creeping up on you, this is worth a short conversation. Worst case you keep doing it yourself and lose nothing by asking.

The takeaway Flex the people, not the paperwork, let the headcount move up and down while someone else carries the payroll underneath.

Frequently asked questions

Can we scale contract care staff up and down easily?

Yes, that is the point of contract staffing. The catch is payroll, which does not scale down as neatly. Putting the employment with an employer of record lets you move headcount freely while the admin stays constant for you, which is one invoice.

Do we pay CPF for contract nurses and care staff?

For employees who are Citizens or PRs, yes. Not for workers on work passes. Pass holders come with their own obligations like levies instead. Confirm current rates and quotas with MOM and the CPF Board.

Why not just hire HR to handle it?

Because the workload spikes and fades. Experienced payroll HR will not take a short contract, and a permanent HR hire is a lot of fixed cost for work that comes in waves. Outsourcing the payroll fits the shape of the problem better.

What happens when the surge ends?

You wind the contract headcount down and it winds down cleanly. As the employer of record we handle the offboarding admin, so there are no trailing payslips or filings left on your desk.

How many workers does this make sense for?

It helps even at a handful, because the risk per worker does not shrink with volume. It helps most when your roster is mixed and your start dates keep moving, which is where in-house admin tends to slip.

What does it cost?

For most homes, near zero on the payroll side, because we run payroll free for the first year. You pay for care hours, not for the admin stacked on top.
