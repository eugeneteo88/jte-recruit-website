How to staff a healthcare business in Singapore: the complete guide

Staffing healthcare here is brutal right now. Here’s the whole picture in one place — and what actually helps at each step.

Eugene Teo

Co-Founder, JTE Recruit · 15 December 2026 · 9 min read

Short answer: staffing a healthcare business in Singapore is genuinely hard right now — but the employers who cope well do a handful of things the strugglers don’t. They understand why the pool is so tight, hire for the setting and not just the title, plan properly for the registration and work-pass maze, treat retention as seriously as recruitment, and use flexible cover for the gaps. This is the complete guide, pulling the whole picture together with a deep-dive on each part. As a recruiter who staffs this space, this is the map I’d hand any healthcare employer feeling stretched.

Work through it in order, or jump to whatever’s hurting most. The one theme running through all of it: in a shortage this deep, every good person you find and keep matters more than usual.

1. Understand the crunch you’re hiring into

An ageing population, a global shortage, and burnout that keeps leaking experienced people out of the field — you can’t fix the macro picture, but understanding it changes how you hire and why retention matters so much. The full picture: Singapore’s healthcare manpower crunch.

2. Hire for the setting, not just the title

A nurse or care worker in a GP clinic, a hospital ward and a nursing home are doing genuinely different jobs — different pace, skills and temperament — even with an identical CV. Matching the person to the environment avoids a quiet, costly mismatch: staffing a clinic vs hospital vs nursing home.

“In a shortage this deep, every good person you find and keep matters more than usual.”

3. Plan for the registration maze

Nurses must be registered with the Singapore Nursing Board; regulated allied health professionals with the AHPC. It’s not a formality, and it’s the thing that makes hires take longer than employers expect. The two deep-dives: why hiring a nurse takes longer than you think (SNB) and the AHPC check you can’t skip.

4. Get foreign hires right

Every foreign healthcare hire faces two hurdles at once — professional registration and a work pass — which is why the perfect candidate can’t start next week. Plan the pipeline: why your foreign nurse can’t start next week, alongside our work-pass guide.

5. Treat retention as your best staffing strategy

In a leaking pool, the cheapest hire is the good nurse or care worker you don’t lose. Burnout, feeling unseen, pay that fell behind — the drivers are fixable, and holding people is far cheaper than a constant hiring war: why your care staff keep leaving.

6. Use flexible cover for the gaps

You don’t need a permanent hire for every gap. Locum and contract cover keeps you running through a shortage, a leave period or a demand spike without over-committing while the market’s this tight — the same logic as contract vs permanent anywhere. Permanent for the core, flexible for the peaks.

7. Know when to get help

You can do all of this yourself, but a lot of it — reaching the people who aren’t applying, planning registration timelines, filling gaps fast — is exactly where a recruiter earns their keep. Helping Singapore healthcare employers staff up, and keep who they’ve got, is the core of our healthcare recruitment.

Frequently asked questions

How do I staff a healthcare business in Singapore?

Understand the shortage you’re hiring into, hire for the specific setting (clinic, hospital or nursing home) not just the title, plan properly for professional registration and work passes, treat retention as seriously as recruitment, and use locum or contract cover for gaps. Reaching people who aren’t applying is often the missing piece.

Why is healthcare so hard to staff right now?

An ageing population is driving demand up fast, the shortage is global so good people have options everywhere, and burnout keeps pushing experienced staff out of the field. The pool is small and leaking — which is why keeping your existing staff matters as much as hiring new ones.

What registration do healthcare workers in Singapore need?

Nurses must be registered (RN) or enrolled (EN) with the Singapore Nursing Board; regulated allied health professionals — physiotherapists, occupational therapists, speech-language therapists, diagnostic radiographers and radiation therapists — must be registered with the AHPC and hold a valid Practising Certificate. Foreign hires also need a work pass. Always confirm current requirements.

How long does it take to hire foreign healthcare staff?

Months rather than weeks, because two systems run in parallel — professional registration (with a licensure or qualifying exam, and probation for nurses) and a Ministry of Manpower work pass. Start early, and favour candidates who are further along or already in Singapore.

How do I keep healthcare staff from leaving?

Protect people from unmanageable workloads, recognise their work genuinely, keep pay honest to the market, offer a path to grow, and be fair and flexible with rostering. Most care staff leave for burnout, feeling unseen or pay that fell behind — not money alone — and most of the fixes are cheaper than replacing them.
