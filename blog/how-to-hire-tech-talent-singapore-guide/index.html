How to hire tech talent in Singapore: the complete employer’s guide

The whole tech-hiring journey in one place — from your first hire to cybersecurity — with links to the deep-dive on each step.

Eugene Teo

Co-Founder, JTE Recruit · 28 November 2026 · 9 min read

Short answer: hiring tech talent well in Singapore comes down to a few honest decisions — and non-tech businesses trip on them most. Get your first hire right, pay to the real market (not the scary headline number), decide whether to build in-house or outsource, pick the right engagement, judge people on real work even when you’re not technical yourself, handle cybersecurity properly, and keep the good ones once you’ve got them. This is the complete guide, and it links to a deep-dive on each step. As a recruiter who also builds software myself, this is the map I’d hand any employer making their move into tech.

Work through it in order or jump to your sticking point. The throughline: tech hiring feels intimidating from the outside, but it’s a series of judgement calls you can absolutely make — with the right questions and, where you need it, a second opinion.

1. Get your first tech hire right

Your first proper tech hire, when nobody in the building is technical, is one of the easiest to get wrong — because you’re judging something you can’t fully judge. Decide whether you need a builder, a maintainer or a decider, and don’t try to buy all three in one junior salary. Full playbook: your first tech hire at a non-tech company.

2. Understand what tech talent actually costs

The numbers you hear feel mad, thanks to the boom and now AI — but “market” isn’t the loudest offer, it’s what the role is genuinely worth. Learn to read it before you post: why tech salaries feel impossible — and what’s actually market.

“Tech hiring feels intimidating from the outside, but it’s a series of judgement calls you can make.”

3. Decide: hire in-house, or outsource?

Hire in-house when software is core and you’ll keep changing it; outsource for a one-off or to move fast — and mind the hidden costs of each. The build-vs-buy call: hire developers or outsource the software.

4. Pick the right engagement

Full-time for ongoing core work, contract for a project, freelance for small bounded tasks — and don’t forget to nail down who owns the IP. The full breakdown: contract, freelance or full-time.

5. Judge developers on real work, even if you’re not technical

Whiteboard puzzles test nerves; real, shipped work and a good conversation test ability — and you can read most of it without being a developer. How to do it: how to tell if a developer is actually good, and the interview signals in interviewing when you’re not technical.

6. Handle cybersecurity properly

Security is the tightest tech market — and “cybersecurity” is really three different jobs. Work out which you need before you post: hiring cybersecurity talent when everyone’s fighting for it.

7. An honest take on the IT market

The IT market moves insanely fast, and AI made it faster. My personal, been-there view on hiring tech people: hiring IT talent in Singapore.

8. Keep the good ones

Tech people are courted constantly, so retention is half the battle. The same truths as everywhere apply — growth, manager, recognition over money alone — and a panic counter-offer usually backfires: why your people keep leaving and should you counter-offer.

9. Know when to get help

You can do all of this yourself — but if you can’t judge the technical side, or the good people simply aren’t applying, that’s where we come in. Helping Singapore employers hire the right tech people is the core of our IT & tech recruitment.

Frequently asked questions

How do I hire tech talent in Singapore?

Get your first hire right (builder vs maintainer vs decider), pay to the genuine market rather than the scary headline number, decide whether to build in-house or outsource, pick the right engagement (full-time, contract or freelance), judge people on real shipped work even if you’re not technical, and keep the good ones once hired. Reaching passive candidates is often the missing piece.

How much does tech talent cost in Singapore?

It varies hugely by skill scarcity, seniority and domain, and the loud top-end figures are usually outliers, not the going rate. AI has pushed genuinely scarce, senior skills up and made routine coding cheaper. Benchmark to what a competent person with that specific skill actually moves for, plus employer on-costs.

Should I hire developers or outsource the software?

Hire in-house when software is core and you’ll keep changing it; outsource for a one-off build or to move fast without a permanent team. Many SMEs outsource to build the first version, then hire in-house to own and evolve it once it’s proven and central.

How do I judge a developer if I’m not technical?

Assess what you can read: real shipped work, a plain-English walkthrough of a past project, how they reason through a problem, and whether they own their mistakes. Use a short, job-realistic take-home over abstract puzzles, and bring in a trusted technical second opinion for the deep review.

Why is tech hiring so hard right now?

Demand is high and the best people are usually employed and not applying, while AI has scrambled both skills and salary expectations. The fix is clarity on exactly who you need, a realistic offer, and reaching the passive candidates who aren’t on the job boards at all.
