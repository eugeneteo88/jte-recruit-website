Contract or permanent IT hire? The bit about payroll that tips it

Contract or permanent for that IT role? Here's the payroll, cost and flexibility difference, and the part that often tips the decision.

Eugene Teo

Co-Founder, JTE Recruit · 16 November 2026 · 6 min read

It is one of the most common calls an IT team makes: do we hire this role permanent, or bring someone on contract? Both can be right. It depends on the work, the certainty, and the budget.

But there is one factor that tends to get left out of the decision until late: the payroll. And once you look at it, it often tips the call.

When permanent makes sense

If the work is ongoing and core, and you are confident it will still be there in two years, permanent is usually the right home for it. You get commitment, continuity, and someone who grows with the team.

The trade-off is that it is a fixed cost. You carry the salary, and the payroll admin, whether the pipeline is full or quiet.

When contract wins

If the work is project-shaped, uncertain, or you need a specialist for a defined stretch, contract fits better. You bring the skill in when you need it and you are not carrying a permanent cost when you do not.

Tech leans this way a lot, because so much of the work genuinely is project-based or spiky.

The payroll admin is the same either way

Here is the bit people miss. Whether the person is permanent or contract, if they are on your books it is the same payroll job, CPF, payslips, the right pass, the filings, the offboarding at the end. Contract does not make the admin lighter.

So the real question is not just contract versus permanent, it is who carries the payroll either way, and whether that is a good use of your people.

Contract through us: flexible and off your plate

This is what tips it for a lot of teams. If you go contract, you can have the contractor join our company as the employer of record. We run their payroll, CPF, passes and filings; you manage the work. It flexes with the project and it is off your plate.

And for most employers it is near-zero cost, we run payroll free for the first year. So contract stops being the option with the messier admin, and becomes the clean, flexible one.

How I would decide

Simple rule of thumb: if the work is core and certain, lean permanent. If it is project-shaped or uncertain, lean contract, and park the payroll with us so the admin is not a reason to avoid it.

If you are weighing a specific role, have a chat with us. No obligation, and we'll be honest about which way fits.

The takeaway Contract does not make payroll lighter, it is the same admin. The real question is who carries it, and whether that is your team's time well spent.

Frequently asked questions

Is a contractor cheaper than a permanent hire?

It depends on the rate, but the real saving is flexibility, you are not carrying a fixed cost when the work is quiet. The payroll admin, though, is similar either way unless you hand it off.

Does contract staffing mean more payroll hassle?

Not if you park it with us. The contractor goes onto our company as the employer of record and we run the payroll, CPF, passes and filings.

What does contract via you cost?

For most employers, near-zero, we run payroll free for the first year. The admin comes off your plate without a new overhead.

Can we convert a contractor to permanent later?

Yes, plenty of teams trial someone on contract and bring them permanent if it works out. We just handle the employment while they are contract.

What about foreign IT contractors?

We handle the pass and filings where they apply. Confirm current eligibility and quota with MOM for your case.

How do we decide for a specific role?

Have a quick, no-obligation chat, we'll talk through the work and be honest about whether contract or permanent fits, and how to keep the payroll simple either way.
