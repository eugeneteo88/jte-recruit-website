5 questions to ask before you work with a recruiter

ET
Eugene Teo

Co-Founder, JTE Recruit · 7 July 2026 · 4 min read

A good recruiter can be the fastest way into a better job. They know which companies are hiring, what those companies actually want, and how to get your CV past the pile and in front of a real person. The wrong recruiter, on the other hand, mostly wastes your afternoon. The good news is you can usually tell which one you're dealing with in the first ten minutes, just by asking a few straight questions.

1Is this free for me?

It should be. In Singapore, a legitimate recruitment agency is paid by the employer who hires, never by the candidate. If anyone asks you for a deposit, a "processing fee", or payment to unlock tasks, that isn't a recruiter, it's a scam. (We wrote a whole guide on spotting those, and you can always check a number here.)

2Are you licensed, and what's your licence number?

Every proper agency in Singapore holds an Employment Agency licence from the Ministry of Manpower. A genuine recruiter will happily give you the number without blinking. Ours is 14C7215, for the record. If the question makes someone uncomfortable, that tells you something.

3Which companies and roles are you actually recruiting for?

A recruiter who knows their patch can name the kind of clients and roles they work on. A recruiter who says "lots of great opportunities" and nothing else is probably fishing. You want specifics: the industry, the seniority, the sort of company. It's a fair question, and the answer tells you whether they can genuinely help you.

4What happens to my CV and my details?

Your CV shouldn't be forwarded to twenty companies the moment you hit send. A good recruiter asks your permission before sharing you with any specific employer, and handles your data properly. Ask how they store it and who sees it. (If you're curious how we handle yours, it's in our privacy policy.)

5Will you prep me and give me feedback?

This is the one that separates a good recruiter from a middleman. The best ones brief you before an interview, tell you what the hiring manager is really looking for, and come back with honest feedback afterwards, even when it's a no. That feedback is gold for your next interview. If a recruiter goes silent the moment they've sent your CV, they were never really in your corner.

The quiet test behind all five: does this person seem interested in your career, or just in filling a seat this week? The right recruiter is playing the long game with you. That's the whole point of the relationship.
