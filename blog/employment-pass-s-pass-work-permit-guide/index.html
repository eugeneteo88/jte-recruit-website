Employment Pass vs S Pass vs Work Permit: an employer’s guide

Eugene Teo

Co-Founder, JTE Recruit · 28 July 2026 · 6 min read

If you’re hiring someone who isn’t a Singapore citizen or PR, the first thing to sort out is the right work pass. Get it wrong and the hire stalls before day one. Here’s a plain-English guide to the three main passes employers deal with — who each is for, and what to watch for.

One thing up front: the Ministry of Manpower (MOM) updates qualifying salaries, quotas and levies from time to time. Treat this as a map of how the system works, and always check mom.gov.sg for the current figures before you budget or commit.

Employment Pass (EP)

For foreign professionals, managers and executives in higher-skilled roles. Candidates must earn at least a qualifying monthly salary — which rises with age and is set higher for the financial services sector — and, since 2023, pass the points-based COMPASS framework. COMPASS scores factors like salary, qualifications, and how the hire adds to your team’s diversity and local workforce. The upside for employers: there’s no quota and no levy on Employment Passes.

S Pass

For mid-skilled staff such as technicians and associate professionals. It has its own, lower qualifying salary than the EP, and — unlike the EP — it comes with two extra constraints: a quota (a cap on the share of your workforce that can be S Pass holders) and a monthly levy for each holder. Both the quota and the levy vary by sector.

Work Permit

For semi-skilled workers in specific sectors — construction, manufacturing, marine shipyard, process and services. Work Permits are tied to approved source countries, capped by a quota (the Dependency Ratio Ceiling, or DRC) and carry a monthly levy. They also come with a maximum period of employment. This is the pass most often used for higher-volume, hands-on hiring.

The three passes at a glance

Employment Pass S Pass Work Permit
Who it's for Professionals & managers Mid-skilled staff Semi-skilled workers
Quota None Yes (varies by sector) Yes (Dependency Ratio Ceiling)
Monthly levy None Yes Yes
Salary & rules set by MOM (COMPASS applies) MOM MOM

Quotas, levies and qualifying salaries change — check mom.gov.sg for current figures.

Quotas and levies, briefly

Two ideas trip up first-time employers. A quota limits how many S Pass and Work Permit holders you can have relative to your local staff — so hiring foreign workers on these passes often depends on your existing headcount mix. A levy is a monthly fee you pay the government for each of those holders. Employment Passes have neither, which is part of why they’re reserved for higher-skilled, better-paid roles.

Which pass does my hire need?

As a rough guide:

  • Professional, managerial or executive role → Employment Pass
  • Mid-skilled technical role → S Pass
  • Semi-skilled role in an eligible sector → Work Permit

In practice the right answer depends on the role, the salary you’re offering, and your current workforce mix — which is exactly where a quick conversation saves a lot of wasted paperwork.
