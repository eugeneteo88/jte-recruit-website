How to spot recruitment scams impersonating agencies

7 July 2026 · 4 min read

Scammers have started impersonating legitimate recruitment agencies — including JTE Recruit — to target job seekers on WhatsApp. They borrow a real agency's name, and sometimes even a real consultant's photo, to look trustworthy. If you've received an unexpected message offering easy, high-paying work, this guide will help you tell the difference between a genuine recruiter and a scam.

How the scam works

The playbook is remarkably consistent:

1. The approach

You receive an unsolicited WhatsApp (or Telegram) message from someone claiming to be a recruiter. They offer a simple, flexible, high-paying "work-from-home" job — often described as completing "tasks", boosting product orders, or rating videos.

2. The hook

You're asked to complete a few small tasks and make a modest deposit to "activate" or "unlock" them. Crucially, the scammer lets you withdraw a small amount early. That first payout feels real and builds your trust.

3. The trap

To access bigger "commissions", you're pressured to deposit more and more money. The amounts escalate, withdrawals stop working, and eventually the money — and the scammer — disappear.

The red flags

  • They ask you to pay a deposit, top-up, or fee to earn or withdraw money.
  • They offer unusually high pay for simple tasks.
  • They let you withdraw a little early, then pressure you to deposit more.
  • They contacted you out of the blue for a job you never applied for.
  • They use a real consultant's name or photo, but a number you can't verify.
  • They rush you, promise "guaranteed" income, or ask you to keep it confidential.

The golden rule: a genuine recruitment agency will never ask a job seeker to pay money, make a deposit, or complete paid tasks. Recruitment is free for candidates — agencies are paid by employers.

If you think you've been targeted

Act quickly. In Singapore you can:

  • Call the ScamShield Helpline at 1799, or the Police Anti-Scam Hotline at 1800-255-0000.
  • Report scams at scamshield.gov.sg.
  • Block and report the number inside WhatsApp, and tell your bank immediately if you sent money.
  • Let us know at ask@jte.com.sg so we can warn others.

Staying cautious costs you nothing. If something feels too good to be true, it almost always is — verify first, and never pay to get a job.
