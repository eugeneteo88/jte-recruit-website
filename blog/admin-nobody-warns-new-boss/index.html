The admin nobody warns you about when you become the boss

Everyone warns new bosses about customers and cashflow. Nobody mentions the payroll admin that lands on you the moment you hire. Here's the part they skip.

Eugene Teo

Co-Founder, JTE Recruit · 23 October 2026 · 6 min read

When you start a business, everyone's got advice. Watch your cashflow. Look after your customers. Hire slow. All good advice. But there's a whole chunk of the job nobody warns you about, and it shows up the moment you hire your first person: the admin.

Here's the part they skip over.

The moment you hire, you become a payroll department

The day you take on your first employee, you quietly sign up for a second job you didn't apply for. Now you have to pay them properly, which in Singapore means CPF, a proper payslip, the right pass if they're foreign, and keeping up with the filings. Every month, on time, correctly.

Nobody mentions this at the start because it's not the exciting part. But it's real, it's recurring, and getting it wrong has actual consequences.

It's not hard, it's just relentless

The thing about payroll admin isn't that any one piece is difficult. It's that it never stops. Every month it comes around again, whether you're busy, travelling, slammed with a big client, or just tired. The deadline doesn't care what else is going on.

And because it's not hard, it's easy to deprioritise, until the month you're swamped, it slips, and a slip with CPF isn't the kind you want.

It pulls you away from the actual job

Here's the real cost. Every hour you spend as the accidental payroll department is an hour not spent being the boss, winning work, looking after your team, thinking about where the business is going. You started the company to do the thing you're good at, not to file CPF.

The admin quietly eats the exact time and attention the business most needs from you.

You don't have to carry it

The good news nobody mentions either: you don't have to do this part. You can hand the payroll out entirely. Your staff go onto our company as the employer of record, and we run the CPF, payslips, passes and filings. The second job you didn't apply for just goes away.

You stay the boss. You just stop being the payroll department.

And it costs almost nothing

The reason this isn't just for big companies with budgets: it's near-zero cost. For most employers we run payroll free for the first year. So getting rid of the admin nobody warned you about doesn't cost you the thing you're guarding most closely when you're starting out, money.

It's about as easy a problem to make disappear as you'll find.

Something I wish someone had told me

If I could go back and tell a new business owner one thing, it would be this: the admin is coming, it's heavier than you expect, and you don't have to be the one doing it. Sort it early and you'll never miss it.

If you've just hired, or you're about to, have a chat with us. No obligation, just the heads-up nobody gave you.

The takeaway The day you hire, you become an accidental payroll department. You don't have to stay one.

Frequently asked questions

Do I really have to handle CPF and payslips myself?

Legally you're responsible for them, but you don't have to be the one doing the work. Hand the payroll out and it's handled for you, properly and on time.

I've only got one or two staff, is this overkill?

Not at all. One or two staff is exactly when the admin feels most out of place, because it's a whole recurring job for very few people.

What happens if I get payroll wrong?

CPF and filings have deadlines and rules, and mistakes can mean penalties. That's why it's worth getting off your plate before a busy month causes a slip. Confirm specifics with MOM/CPF.

How much does it cost to hand it over?

For most employers, near-zero, we run payroll free for the first year. I'll walk you through it on a quick call, no obligation.

Will I still control pay and hiring decisions?

Completely. You run the business and decide the pay; we just handle the payroll mechanics behind it.

Do you handle foreign staff passes?

Yes, where the passes apply, including the filings. Always confirm current eligibility and quota with MOM for your case.
