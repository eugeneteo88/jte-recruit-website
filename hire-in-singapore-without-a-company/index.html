The short answer

Can You Legally Hire Without a Local Company?

Yes, you can. The catch people trip over is this: in Singapore, someone has to be the legal employer on paper, and that employer has to meet CPF, tax and work pass rules. If you don't have your own company here, you can't be that employer yourself. So you borrow one.

That's exactly what an Employer of Record does. A licensed local firm, us in this case, signs the employment contract, pays the person, sorts their CPF and taxes, and sponsors their work pass if they're a foreigner. On paper the person is employed by that firm. In practice they report to you, do your work, and you decide their pay and their role.

It's a normal, above-board arrangement. Nothing grey about it. The person is properly employed, properly paid, properly covered. You just skipped the part where you spend weeks and money registering a company you might not even need yet.

How it actually works

How Do You Employ Without Your Own Entity? The EOR Route

Four steps, and you never touch ACRA.

  1. You pick the person and the pay

    Your hire, your decision, your salary. Local or foreign, your first person here or your fifteenth. If you still need to find them, that's our main job too.

  2. We become the employer on paper

    They sign an employment contract with us. We're the legal employer under our licensed Singapore entity, so you don't need one of your own.

  3. We run everything each month

    Payroll, employer CPF, SDL, payslips, year-end IR8A, and the work pass application, renewal or cancellation if it's a foreign hire. All filed against current MOM, CPF and IRAS rules.

  4. You get one simple invoice

    We bill you for the salary plus the statutory costs. You direct the work day to day. That's it. No HR team, no filings, no entity to maintain.

This is the same thing people call payroll and work pass outsourcing once you do have a company. Before you have one, it's employer of record. Same engine.

For a foreign hire

What About Work Passes for a Foreign Hire?

This is where an EOR really earns its keep. A Singapore work pass is tied to the employing entity, not to the person. No local employer, no pass. So if you want to bring in a foreigner and you don't have a company here, an EOR is often the only clean way to do it: we hold the pass as the legal employer, and your hire works for you.

The two passes you'll usually meet have minimum salary floors you need to clear:

Employment Pass (EP)

For professionals and managers. Qualifying salary is S$5,600 now, rising to S$6,000 from January 2027. Older candidates need more.

S Pass

For mid-skilled staff. Minimum salary is S$3,300 now, rising to S$3,600. Comes with quota and levy for the employer.

Figures are a guide, confirm the current numbers with MOM before you plan a salary around them. We keep on top of these so you don't have to.

We handle the application, the renewals and the cancellation when someone moves on. If you want the full picture on passes, we wrote a plain-English guide to the EP, S Pass and Work Permit.

How fast you can start

Entity Setup Time vs Starting Through an EOR

If speed matters, this is the deciding bit. Registering your own Pte Ltd is only the first step. After ACRA you still need a corporate secretary, a corporate bank account, accounting set up, and then the work pass on top if your hire is a foreigner. Realistically that's weeks, sometimes longer when a bank account drags.

Through an EOR there's no company to register, so we can usually get someone onto payroll in days once we've agreed terms and have their details. A work pass still takes its own processing time, but you're not waiting on a whole corporate setup before the clock even starts. Plenty of founders start through an EOR to get their first person working, then spin up their own entity later once the market's proven.

The honest take

When Should I Set Up My Own Entity Instead?

An EOR is brilliant for getting started and for staying lean, but it isn't forever for everyone. I'd rather tell you straight when it's time to incorporate than keep you on a service you've outgrown.

Set up your own Singapore company when it's becoming a real base: you're growing a sizeable local team, you need to sign contracts, raise money or hold assets through a Singapore entity, you want HR fully in-house, or the economics simply tip once you're past a handful of people. At that point your own Pte Ltd makes sense.

Until then, starting through an EOR lets you test the market with real people on the ground and almost no downside. A lot of companies do exactly that, then move to their own entity when they're sure. Whenever that switch makes sense for you, I'll say so.

The EOR carries the compliance so you don’t need your own Singapore entity.

Is hiring through an EOR legal in Singapore?+

Yes, it’s a normal and legal arrangement. The person is properly employed under a licensed company that meets its MOM, CPF and IRAS obligations. JTE has run this as a licensed agency since 2014.
