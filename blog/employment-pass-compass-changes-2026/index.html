The Employment Pass keeps getting more expensive — here’s how to plan for 2027

You found the right person and budgeted for them — then discovered the EP salary floor and COMPASS make it cost more. And the bar rises again in 2027. This is how to plan it, not panic over it.

Eugene Teo

Co-Founder, JTE Recruit · 18 October 2026 · 7 min read

Here’s the scenario we keep seeing. An employer finds the right foreign hire, agrees a salary, and builds the budget around it — then runs the numbers against the Employment Pass rules and realises the pass alone costs more than planned. The salary floor is higher than they remembered, the COMPASS points test adds another hurdle, and the figures are set to rise again in 2027. It’s a frustrating place to land a good hire. The fix isn’t to panic — it’s to plan to the current bar and check it early. Let me walk through what’s actually changed and what to do about it.

None of this is a reason to stop hiring the people you need. It’s a reason to put the pass cost into the plan from day one, so it never ambushes you at offer stage or, worse, at renewal. (Figures below are what we understand to be current — always confirm current figures with MOM before acting.)

The salary floor is higher than most employers remember

Since 1 January 2025 for new applications (1 January 2026 for renewals), the EP minimum qualifying salary is S$5,600 a month for most sectors and S$6,200 a month for financial services. The catch that surprises people: those are the floor for the youngest applicants — roughly age 23 — and the required salary rises progressively with age. By age 45 and above, the benchmark climbs to around S$10,700 a month for non-financial roles and about S$11,800 a month for financial services.

So the mistake isn’t usually the headline number — it’s assuming the headline number applies to your candidate. An experienced mid-career specialist sits well above the entry floor, and that is often where a budget quietly breaks. Confirm current figures with MOM before you commit to an offer.

COMPASS is a second hurdle, not a formality

Meeting the salary floor gets you to the start line, not over it. Most EP applications also have to clear COMPASS, the points-based framework MOM uses to assess them. In general an application needs to score at least 40 points to pass, unless it is exempt. Applicants with a fixed monthly salary of at least S$22,500 are exempt from COMPASS.

For most everyday hires, you are well below that exemption, so COMPASS applies. It’s worth understanding how the points work before you fall in love with a candidate — our guide to the Employment Pass, S Pass and Work Permit lays out how the passes differ and where each fits. As always, confirm the current criteria with MOM.

“The bar moving isn’t the problem. Finding out at renewal is.”

The bar rises again in 2027

At Committee of Supply 2026 it was announced that the EP minimum qualifying salary will rise to S$6,000 a month for most sectors and S$6,600 a month for financial services. Two dates matter here, and they’re different on purpose:

  • New applications: the higher floor applies from 1 January 2027.
  • Renewals: the higher floor applies to passes expiring from 1 January 2028.

That gap is a planning window, not a loophole. If you’re hiring new in 2027, budget to the new floor. If you have holders whose passes run into 2028, you have time to get their pay and their COMPASS position in order before it bites. Confirm current figures with MOM before acting.

Your existing EP holder can be affected at renewal

This is the part that catches employers off guard. EP renewals are assessed against the prevailing criteria — the rules in force at the time of renewal, not the rules that applied when the pass was first granted. So someone you hired comfortably a few years ago, whose salary has barely moved since, can find themselves under the current floor when their pass comes up for renewal.

The practical move is boring but it works: take your list of EP holders, note each expiry date, and check each person’s current salary against the requirement that will apply at their renewal — factoring in both the age-based benchmark and the 2027 uplift. Where someone is close to the line, you have a decision to make early, calmly, with a salary review in hand — rather than a scramble weeks before expiry.

So what should an employer actually do?

Three habits keep this from becoming a fire drill:

  • Budget to the real number, by age. Don’t anchor on the entry floor. Work out the benchmark for your actual candidate’s profile, add the 2027 figure if the start date lands there, and build the offer around that.
  • Check renewals a year out. Run the EP-holder list against prevailing criteria annually. A quiet salary review now is far cheaper than losing a trained person at renewal.
  • Ask whether the role needs a pass at all. For some roles, a strong local hire removes the salary floor, COMPASS and renewal risk in one move — and can open the door to hiring and training support.

Don’t forget the local-talent option

When the pass math gets tight, it’s worth genuinely asking whether the role has to be filled by a foreign hire. For some positions the honest answer is no — and a capable local candidate sidesteps the floor, the points test and the renewal uncertainty entirely. It also sits better with the ratios you’re allowed to run; our explainer on how many foreign workers you can hire is a useful sense-check before you commit to the EP route.

And where you’re developing local talent into the role, some of that cost doesn’t have to be yours alone — our guide to Singapore hiring grants covers the schemes that can part-fund it. The point isn’t foreign-versus-local as a principle; it’s matching each role to the route that actually makes sense for the work and the budget.

The bottom line

The Employment Pass is getting more expensive, and 2027 pushes it up again — but none of it is a surprise if you plan for it. Budget to the age-adjusted floor, not the headline. Check your renewals against the prevailing rules a year out. And keep the local-talent option genuinely on the table. Do that and the rising bar becomes a line item you’ve already accounted for, instead of a shock at offer stage.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Employment Pass minimum qualifying salary in 2026?

Since 1 January 2025 for new applications (1 January 2026 for renewals), the EP minimum qualifying salary is S$5,600 a month for most sectors and S$6,200 a month for financial services. Those figures are the floor for the youngest applicants (around age 23) and rise progressively with age — up to about S$10,700 (non-financial) and S$11,800 (financial services) at age 45 and above. Always confirm current figures with MOM before acting.

What is changing for the Employment Pass in 2027?

Announced at Committee of Supply 2026, the EP minimum qualifying salary will rise to S$6,000 a month for most sectors and S$6,600 a month for financial services. The higher floor applies to new EP applications from 1 January 2027, and to renewals of passes expiring from 1 January 2028. Confirm current figures with MOM before acting.

What is COMPASS and how does it affect my hire?

COMPASS is the points-based framework MOM uses to assess most EP applications. On top of meeting the salary floor, an application generally needs to score at least 40 points (unless exempt). Applicants with a fixed monthly salary of at least S$22,500 are exempt from COMPASS. Confirm current figures and criteria with MOM before acting.

Will my existing EP holder be affected at renewal?

Possibly. EP renewals are assessed against the prevailing (updated) criteria, not the rules that applied when the pass was first granted. So an existing holder whose pay sits near the old floor could fall short at renewal as the bar moves. Check each person’s salary against the current requirement well ahead of their expiry date, and confirm current figures with MOM.

Is there a way to hire that avoids the EP entirely?

Sometimes. If the role can be filled by a local candidate, you avoid the pass, the salary floor and COMPASS altogether — and you may qualify for hiring and training support. For roles that genuinely need a foreign specialist, the better move is to budget for the current bar and plan renewals early rather than hope the figures hold.
