What is payroll outsourcing?

Payroll outsourcing is simply when you hand your monthly payroll run to an outside provider instead of doing it yourself. Every month the provider takes your headcount and hours, works out each person's net pay, CPF and the Skills Development Levy, produces the payslips, and submits the contributions and filings to the CPF Board and IRAS. You approve the numbers, they do the rest.

In Singapore the reason people do this is less about saving on salaries and more about getting the compliance right. Employer CPF, SDL, IR8A, IR21 tax clearance, work pass admin, there are a lot of moving parts, and the deadlines are hard. One late or short submission and you are into interest and penalties. Outsourcing moves all of that onto someone who does it every single day.

Worth clearing up one thing: payroll outsourcing is the same core job whatever your sector. An IT team, a clinic, a logistics crew, the payroll mechanics are identical. What actually drives the work is how many people you have and what their contracts say, not the industry you are in.

The monthly rhythm

How does it work month to month?

Once you are set up, a normal month looks like this. Most of it happens without you lifting a finger.

1

You send any changes

New joiners, leavers, overtime, bonuses, unpaid leave, claims. If nothing changed, you send nothing and the run just repeats.

2

We calculate the run

Gross to net, employer and employee CPF, SDL, pro-rated pay for part-month joiners. You get a summary to approve before anyone is paid.

3

Staff get paid and payslipped

Salaries go out by your pay date, each person gets an itemised payslip (which is a legal requirement here under the Employment Act).

4

CPF and levies are filed

CPF contributions are submitted and paid to the CPF Board. CPF is due by the 14th of the following month, so this is the deadline we work backwards from every cycle.

5

Year-end and exits are handled

IR8A goes to IRAS at year-end, and when a foreign employee leaves we handle IR21 tax clearance so their final pay clears cleanly.

The money question

How much does payroll outsourcing cost in Singapore?

Most providers charge a small monthly fee per employee. As a defensible range to plan around, think roughly S$10 to S$30 per employee per month, often with a minimum monthly charge for very small teams (always confirm the exact quote, since pricing varies by provider and scope). On top of the fee you still pay the actual statutory costs, which are not the provider's charge but your own obligation as the employer.

The bit people underestimate is the statutory layer. On a typical salary in the S$800 to S$4,500 band, you are looking at employer CPF of 17% plus SDL of 0.25%, so about 17.25% on top of gross pay going to the CPF Board. That is your cost either way, in-house or outsourced. The outsourcing fee just buys you the admin, the compliance and the deadlines being someone else's problem.

The data question

Is it safe to hand over payroll data?

Fair question, and the short answer is yes, as long as you pick a licensed, PDPA-compliant provider. Payroll data (salaries, NRIC, bank details) is personal data under Singapore's Personal Data Protection Act, so a proper provider is legally required to protect it, limit who can see it, and use it only for running your payroll.

What to actually check before you hand anything over: is the provider licensed, do they have a named Data Protection Officer, how do they store and transmit your data, and can they give you references. At JTE we have run payroll since 2014, we keep payroll data private, and we have a DPO appointed. That part is not negotiable for us.

Right fit

When does it make sense?

You do not need to be big. In practice, outsourcing pays off the moment payroll starts costing you real time or real risk.

Small businesses

You are too small to justify a full HR hire, but payroll and CPF are still eating a day or two a month. Hand it over and get the time back.

Project & contract teams

Headcount scales up for a project then winds down. A variable per-head fee fits that far better than a fixed in-house cost.

Teams with foreign staff

Work passes, levies and IR21 clearance add a compliance layer. If you have staff on passes, outsourcing takes the risk off your desk.

Mid-year moves

How to switch providers mid-year

You can switch any time, you are not locked into the calendar. The one thing that matters is a clean handover of your year-to-date figures, so CPF contributions and IR8A totals still add up correctly at year-end. Get that right and a mid-year switch is a non-event for your staff.

Pull your last few months of payslips and CPF submission history.

Hand over employee records: salaries, pass details, bank details, leave balances.

The incoming provider reconciles the year-to-date totals so nothing double-counts.

Agree the first cycle they will run, and confirm the old provider's last one.

We do this handover all the time and it is genuinely painless. Most clients switch between two pay cycles without their staff noticing a thing.
